The €15k wedge offer is the **AgOps Design Sprint** (`/audit` public teaser, `/offers/audit` gated brief, `public/downloads/liza-os-ai-opportunity-audit.pdf`). It is positioned as paid architecture work, not as an audit or neutral diagnostic.

**Canonical names:**
- Offer: "AgOps Design Sprint" (preferred) / "AI Opportunity Audit" (legacy URL/SEO only)
- Methodology: "Agentic Operations (AgOps)" — always introduce on first use; never assume the buyer knows the term
- Solution: "LIZA OS" as the active enforcement layer

**Forbidden phrasing** (do not reintroduce):
- "product-agnostic"
- "LIZA OS is one option" / "never the conclusion"
- "vendor-neutral"
- "the audit stands alone, you are under no obligation"
- Framing the deliverable as an "audit report" or "forensic exercise" — it is a blueprint/architecture
- Any framing that lets the buyer take the roadmap to an internal team or another vendor without friction.

**Required stance:**
- We have a strong point of view: an active enforcement layer is the only thing that makes AgOps possible at scale, and LIZA OS is the infrastructure we built for it.
- The sprint proves the necessity of this architecture on the buyer's data; the deployment build is the self-evident next step.
- "We are not here to give you options. We are here to design the only operational architecture we believe works."

**Post-sprint pricing (value-anchored, never flat):**
- Always tie post-sprint pricing to the cost-of-inaction surfaced in the diagnostic (€400k–€700k/yr typical).
- Phrase: "priced to deliver a minimum 3x ROI on the hidden cost we identify, typically €35k–€50k for the initial 3-workflow enforcement build."
- Never quote a flat "€25k deployment" — it deflates the narrative.

**Why:** "Audit" anchors buyers to a PDF report; "Design Sprint" anchors them to architecture and a build. AgOps directly addresses the Head of AI's actual nightmare — change management and human–agent handoffs — which static docs cannot govern, making LIZA OS inevitable rather than optional.
