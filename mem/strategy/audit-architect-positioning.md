The €15k wedge offer is the **AI Execution Blueprint** (`/audit` public teaser, `/offers/audit` gated brief, `public/downloads/liza-os-ai-opportunity-audit.pdf`). It is positioned as paid mapping and architecture work for the buyer's executable knowledge layer, not as an audit or neutral diagnostic.

**Canonical names:**
- Offer: "AI Execution Blueprint" (preferred). "AgOps Design Sprint" and "AI Opportunity Audit" are deprecated; only the URL/PDF filename remain for backwards compatibility.
- Central noun: **executable knowledge** / **the executable knowledge layer**. This matches the homepage and Capabilities section vocabulary already in production.
- Solution: "LIZA OS" as the active enforcement layer for executable knowledge.
- "AgOps" / "Agentic Operations" may appear at most parenthetically on internal/sales-led conversations. Do not lead with it on public pages — it is jargon the buyer does not yet share.

**The central claim (must appear on both /audit and /offers/audit):**
Every part of an AI stack — LLMs, RAG, prompt libraries, agents, operators, tools — needs the same input to work as a system: a clear, governed, current definition of how the company decides (quality standards, decision logic, playbooks). That layer is **executable knowledge**. Without it, every AI investment stays disconnected. With it, they all compound around a single source of judgment. The blueprint maps this layer; LIZA OS deploys and enforces it.

**Forbidden phrasing** (do not reintroduce):
- "product-agnostic"
- "LIZA OS is one option" / "never the conclusion"
- "vendor-neutral"
- "the audit stands alone, you are under no obligation"
- Framing the deliverable as an "audit report" or "forensic exercise" — it is a blueprint/architecture
- Aspirational, abstract phrasing ("operational architecture for the agentic age", "2026 capabilities on 2015 infrastructure"). Use the site's plain operational voice: short sentences, no em-dashes, name the symptom directly.
- Any framing that lets the buyer take the roadmap to an internal team or another vendor without friction.

**Required stance:**
- We have a strong point of view: the executable knowledge layer cannot be governed or enforced by reactive tools (Notion, Slack, ChatGPT). LIZA OS is the active enforcement layer we built for it.
- The sprint proves the necessity of this layer on the buyer's data; the build is the obvious next step.
- We tell the buyer where to put their AI budget and where to stop spending. Direct, not neutral.

**Post-sprint pricing (value-anchored, never flat):**
- Always tie post-sprint pricing to the cost-of-inaction surfaced in the diagnostic (€400k–€700k/yr typical).
- Phrase: "priced to deliver a minimum 3x ROI on the hidden cost we identify, typically €35k–€50k for the initial 3-workflow enforcement build."
- Never quote a flat "€25k deployment" — it deflates the narrative.

**Why:** "Audit" anchors buyers to a PDF report. "Blueprint" anchors them to a build. Centering the offer on **executable knowledge** (the site's existing vocabulary) is more concrete than "AgOps" jargon, plugs directly into the Head of AI's nightmare (change management, human-agent handoffs, drift, lost expertise), and makes LIZA OS inevitable rather than optional because nothing else governs that layer.
